Every fixture below is one where our number and the bookmakers' price disagree by at least 5 points on some outcome. For each, the gap is split in two: the ratings-only forecast (plain attack/defence plus the Dixon-Coles grid, no other adjustments) and the published one (after the referee, rest, travel and availability nudges). If the two are close together, the disagreement is a recent-form call — our ratings price the match differently from the market. If the published bar has swung away from the ratings-only one, the adjustments are doing the disagreeing. Market = the pre-match price with the bookmaker's margin removed.

Not a betting recommendation. Ratings come from historical scoring data (expected goals where available, shots on target otherwise), time-decayed; matches are priced with a Poisson-based method (Dixon & Coles, 1997) plus a whole-diagonal draw correction. The referee / rest / travel adjustments each individually made the model slightly less accurate in three seasons of walk-forward testing and are kept on by an explicit decision to use more of the signal bookmakers price in — so a disagreement driven by them is the weakest kind. Where the model and the market diverge, the market usually knows something the numbers don't yet (a signing, team news). Full method in the project README.
