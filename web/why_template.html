Every fixture below is one where our number and the bookmakers' price disagree by at least 5 points on some outcome. For each, the gap is split in two: the ratings-only forecast (plain attack/defence plus the Dixon-Coles grid) and the published one. These are usually identical — the referee / rest / travel nudges are off by default (they tested worse), so an adjustment only moves the number on a Premier League fixture with --use-injuries set. When the two bars differ, an adjustment is doing the disagreeing; when they match, it's a pure recent-form call — our ratings price the match differently from the market. Market = the pre-match price with the bookmaker's margin removed.

Not a betting recommendation. Ratings come from historical scoring data (expected goals where available, shots on target otherwise), time-decayed; matches are priced with a Poisson-based method (Dixon & Coles, 1997) plus a whole-diagonal draw correction. The referee / rest / travel adjustments each individually made the model slightly less accurate in three seasons of walk-forward testing, so they're off by default — a disagreement driven by an adjustment is the weakest kind and is now rare. Where the model and the market diverge, the market usually knows something the numbers don't yet (a signing, team news). Full method in the project README.
