Two kinds of evidence, kept apart. The live record is every prediction the site actually published, scored against the result once it's in — first prediction per fixture, never restated with hindsight. It starts near-empty and fills one matchday at a time. The validation backtest is the same model run walk-forward over three seasons of history at its current settings — thousands of matches of context that doesn't depend on how long the live log has been running. The shaded band on the RPS charts is the published range for football forecasts, roughly 0.184–0.213 (Constantinou & Fenton).

Not a betting recommendation. RPS is the ranked probability score (Constantinou & Fenton 2012); lower is better. "Market" is the fixture's pre-match price with the bookmaker's margin removed — drawn as a dashed reference line because it's the benchmark, not a competitor. Significance is a paired Diebold-Mariano test on the per-match RPS difference. The scoreline row is scored on the model's single most-likely score, a by-product of the grid rather than its main output — exact-score accuracy tops out near 12% for anyone, so the "always 1-1" baseline is shown beside it. Colours are checked colourblind-safe; every chart has a table view. Full method in the project README.
