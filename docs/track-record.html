Two kinds of evidence, kept apart. The live record is every prediction the site actually published, scored against the result once it's in — first prediction per fixture, never restated with hindsight. It starts near-empty and fills one matchday at a time. The validation backtest is the same model run walk-forward over three seasons of history at its current settings — thousands of matches of context that doesn't depend on how long the live log has been running.

Not a betting recommendation. RPS is the ranked probability score (Constantinou & Fenton 2012); lower is better, the published band for football is roughly 0.184–0.213. "Market" is the fixture's pre-match price with the bookmaker's margin removed. Significance is a paired Diebold-Mariano test on the per-match RPS difference. The scoreline row is scored on the model's single most-likely score, which is a by-product of the grid, not its main output — exact-score accuracy tops out near 12% for anyone, so the "always 1-1" baseline is shown next to it.
