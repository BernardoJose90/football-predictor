Every fixture below got two forecasts: ours, built from each team's recent attack and defence (expected goals where available, shots on target otherwise), with a correction that boosts every draw scoreline — not just 0-0 — since plain models are known to underrate how often matches end level — and the market's, the bookmakers' own price with the margin stripped out. Premier League fixtures also factor in each squad's current injury/availability picture, pulled live from the Premier League's own Fantasy Football data — flagged in orange below, and still an open experiment, not a proven improvement. A team without enough matches in this league yet is left unrated rather than guessed at. Where the two forecasts land close together, that's the model's reasoning holding up against people paid to get this right. Where they don't, the market usually knows something we don't — a signing, team news that isn't in the numbers yet.

Not a betting recommendation. This model rates teams from historical scoring data and prices matches with a Poisson-based method (Dixon & Coles, 1997) plus a whole-diagonal draw correction. Referee, rest-day and travel adjustments were built and tested against three seasons of results; each made the forecasts slightly less accurate, so they're off by default. Premier League fixtures do fold in live squad availability from the Premier League's own Fantasy Football data — the one adjustment here with no historical test behind it at all, since no free record of past injury news exists to check it against; treat it as a live experiment, not a proven edge. A team without enough matches in this league yet is excluded rather than guessed at. It doesn't know about transfers mid-window or breaking team news. Market figures are pre-match prices with the bookmaker's margin removed, for comparison only. Full numbers in the project README.
