Where the two forecasts land close together, that's the model's reasoning holding up against people paid to get this right. Where they don't, the market usually knows something we don't — a signing, team news that isn't in the numbers yet.

The headline number on every fixture is the bookmakers' own price, with the margin stripped out — the sharpest public read there is on who wins. From that same price we solve for the expected goals each side is implied to score and read off a likely scoreline, tightened by the bookmakers' over/under 2.5 line where it's quoted. Underneath, in the thin bar, is our own ratings model — recent attack and defence, expected goals where available — shown purely for contrast: where it pulls well away from the price (flagged ±pt), it's usually the model rating form the market has marked down for a reason the numbers can't see.

Headline figures are pre-match bookmaker prices with the margin removed by simple (multiplicative) devigging; the scoreline comes from a Dixon–Coles Poisson grid (1997) fitted so its own 1X2 and over/under 2.5 probabilities match that price. Reconstructing a grid from a price is lossy — treat the likely score as the centre of a wide range, not a call. The comparison model lands about 1% (RPS) worse than the closing line, which is expected: the closing line is the strongest public forecast there is. Full method in the project README.

Not a betting recommendation. This model rates teams from historical scoring data and prices matches with a Poisson-based method (Dixon & Coles, 1997) plus a whole-diagonal draw correction. Referee, rest-day and travel adjustments were built and tested against three seasons of results; each made the forecasts slightly less accurate, so they're off by default. Premier League fixtures do fold in live squad availability from the Premier League's own Fantasy Football data — the one adjustment here with no historical test behind it at all, since no free record of past injury news exists to check it against; treat it as a live experiment, not a proven edge. A team without enough matches in this league yet is excluded rather than guessed at. It doesn't know about transfers mid-window or breaking team news.
